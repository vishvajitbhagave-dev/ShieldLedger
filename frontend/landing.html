How it works

Get financed in 5 steps.

Three parties, one confidential auction. A shared insurance pool and a private secondary market complete the lifecycle — nothing about your financials is ever public.

01

Register your invoice

Prove your credit score and reputation in zero knowledge. A 2% insurance premium is automatically paid into the shared default pool. Only a nullifier — a blinded hash plus a random secret — is posted on-chain; your invoice details never leave your browser.

02

Get buyer-verified

Your corporate buyer cryptographically confirms the invoice is genuine and owed. Only a Buyer-verified ✓ flag and an opaque commitment become public — identity and terms stay private.

03

Sealed-bid auction

Lenders submit sealed bids — only commitments go on-chain, so no lender sees another's terms. Whoever reveals the lowest interest rate wins.

04

Settle & build reputation

The contract pays the winning lender automatically. Your private reputation updates: +10 for on-time, −20 for late. Lenders see only the proven bound, never the score.

05

Resell or claim insurance

Lenders can privately resell claims to other investors on the secondary market. If an invoice defaults, the claim holder collects from the shared insurance pool — the defaulting SME is never identified.

Security

Zero knowledge by default.

ShieldLedger runs on the Midnight Network, where transactions are validated with zero-knowledge proofs. Here's what that means for your data.

  • Only commitments, never facts. A nullifier — a blinded hash of your invoice details plus a secret — is the only thing posted on-chain.
  • Proven bounds, not scores. Credit and reputation checks prove "score ≥ N" in zero knowledge. The scores themselves are never revealed to anyone.
  • Anonymous buyers. A buyer's identity, other supplier relationships, and full contract terms never appear on-chain — only a ✓ flag and an opaque commitment.
  • Sealed bids. Lenders commit to terms they keep hidden until reveal; no lender sees any other bid before the winner is determined.
  • Anonymous insurance pool. Premiums merge into one shared balance — nothing links an amount to a specific SME. Claims are recorded only under the invoice's public nullifier; the defaulting party is never identified.
  • Private secondary market. Claim transfers replace an opaque commitment on-chain. The investor's identity, the seller's identity, and the number of transfers are never revealed.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What exactly goes on-chain?

An invoice nullifier, the proven lower bounds for credit and reputation, the claimed amount, buyer verification flags, and sealed bid commitments. Your actual credit or reputation score, your identity, invoice reference, due date, and the terms of your contracts never appear on-chain.

How is the lowest rate guaranteed?

Bids are sealed on-chain — lenders post only a commitment to their terms, so no lender can see another bid. Whoever reveals the lowest interest rate wins, and the contract pays that winner automatically.

How does reputation work?

Your reputation is stored in your own private wallet state. Settling on or before the due date earns you +10; a late settlement costs −20, clamped to 0–100. When you register, you prove "score ≥ threshold" in zero knowledge — lenders are bound to what you really have without ever seeing the score.

Who can participate?

Three roles share one workflow: the SME registers invoices and seeks financing, the corporate buyer verifies invoices, and lenders bid to finance them. You'll need the Midnight Lace wallet to sign transactions.

How does the insurance pool work?

Every invoice registration pays a 2% premium into one shared pool. If a financed invoice is never settled and past its due date, the current claim holder can collect 50% of the financed amount from the pool — or whatever remains if the pool is thin. Both percentages are proven in zero knowledge. The defaulting SME's identity is never revealed.

Can I resell my claim on a financed invoice?

Yes. Before settlement, the winning lender can transfer their claim to another investor. Only an opaque commitment goes on-chain — the buyer's identity, the seller's identity, and the number of times the claim was resold are never revealed. After transfer, only the new holder can settle or claim insurance.

Is this a live financial product?

ShieldLedger is a working demonstration running on the Midnight public preview network using testnet funds — not a regulated financial service. It's a real contract with real zero-knowledge proofs, made to show what confidential invoice financing can look like.
